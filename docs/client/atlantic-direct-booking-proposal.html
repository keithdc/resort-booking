1. What we are building

You currently list on Airbnb. That relationship is ending and will not be renewed. This project replaces Airbnb with your own direct-booking website: you own the guest, the calendar, and the payment. It is not a marketplace and not a CRM. One organisation (you) can run many properties, each with many bookable houses.

Guests book in a straight line: dates and guests → free properties → name, email, phone → pay.

Surface Who uses it What it does
Guest website Travellers Photos, map, search, hold dates, pay (card / Apple Pay / Google Pay / bank transfer)
Staff admin You and your team Properties, houses, calendar, extras, refunds, bank-transfer checks, reports
API Both sites Availability, holds, prices, refunds. No booking maths in the website.

What this is not

  • Not Airbnb, Booking.com, or a multi-host marketplace
  • Not in-app guest–host chat (phone and email only)
  • Not PayPal or “pay later” (Klarna and similar) in the first live version
  • Not a package holiday (no flights bundled with the stay)

Launch shape

Two properties at launch, many houses each, more properties later without rebuilding the database. Guest calendars block past days. Staff calendars do not, so you can record history.

3. Requirements (product)

Guest

  • Multi-property catalogue, photos, OpenStreetMap, phone and email
  • Search by check-in, nights, guests; no identity documents stored
  • Hold: 15 minutes (card/wallets) or 48 hours (bank transfer)
  • Warning at five minutes left; expired hold keeps the form
  • Cookie consent before advertising pixels

Staff

  • Add properties and houses without a software release
  • Calendar of who is in-house; full stay (requests, notes, payment)
  • Verify bank receipts; approve on-request extras after the stay is paid
  • Cancellation / rebook settings per property, snapshotted onto the booking
  • Reports: nights, occupancy, revenue, filter by property

Money and EU operations

  • Never store card numbers. Stripe handles SCA / 3-D Secure
  • Bank transfer: unique reference; staff match the bank, not “accept the guest”
  • Dated holiday lodging: the 14-day cooling-off right typically does not apply (Consumer Rights Directive 2011/83/EU Article 16(l)). Checkout must say so clearly.
  • Your cancellation percentages are a commercial policy, not an EU-wide legal table
  • If you cancel the stay, the guest is refunded 100% of what they paid
  • Do not describe prepayment as French arrhes unless your lawyer says to

4. Architecture the board can read

Two websites, one API, one PostgreSQL database (the ledger), Redis only for short holds and jobs. Guest and staff are never one app with a “role switch.”

Piece Choice Why
Guest & admin UI Next.js + React + MUI SEO for listings; dense tools for staff; same language as the API
API NestJS + TypeScript Holds, overlap, refunds live here — not in the browser
Database PostgreSQL Reservations are rows with constraints, not a cache
Holds / jobs Redis 15-minute / 48-hour freezes and emails; never the booking history
Cards / wallets Stripe Apple Pay and Google Pay use the same fee as typing a card
Maps OpenStreetMap Directions via Google/Apple Maps links; no in-app chat

Model: Organisation → Property → House → Reservation. Every property-scoped row stores organisation and property ids so a third resort cannot leak into the first.

Technical guidelines (non-negotiable)

  • Strict TypeScript; no any
  • No availability or refund maths in React
  • Feature folders; every screen has an API and a SQL migration
  • Buttons 8px radius; colours from a theme, not hard-coded per page
  • Hosting, backups, and secrets stay off laptops in production

8. After go-live — watch period and modest retainer

A new booking system should not be left alone the week after the first real guest. The plan is June 2027 go-live after May UAT, then a stabilisation (watch) period of three (3) months, extendable to six (6) months if both Parties agree in the statement of work.

What the watch period covers

  • Monitoring that the site, API, holds, and (once live) Stripe/bank-transfer paths are up
  • Correction of defects in the software that was delivered (bugs, not new features)
  • Help with backups and a restore drill once during the period
  • A short monthly written note: what was watched, what was fixed, what is still on you (content, photos, legal copy)

What it does not cover

  • New properties beyond training you to add them yourselves
  • New product (chat, PayPal, marketplace, mobile apps)
  • Your Stripe KYC, bank, photography, or lawyer
  • Hosting invoices from the cloud provider (those stay in your name)

Indicative retainer (to confirm in the statement of work, ex-VAT): €180 to €320 per month for the watch period — on the order of one mid-week night, not a second build. Three months is the default (July–September 2027 if go-live is June). A further three months is optional if you want a quieter first winter. Either Party may end the optional extension with 30 days’ written notice after the first three months. Hours above a small included allowance (for example four hours per month) are billed at the SOW rate or deferred to a change request — the retainer is meant to sit comfortably, not to surprise you.

Figures are commercial guidance for discussion, not an invoice. Hosting, Stripe fees, and domain renewals are extra and paid by the Operator to those suppliers.

10.2 Definitions

“Confidential Information” means all non-public information, in any form, that a Party (the “Disclosing Party”) discloses to the other (the “Receiving Party”), whether or not marked confidential, including: business plans, occupancy, rates, guest or staff personal data, bank and Stripe details; trademarks, unpublished logos, brand guides, photographs and copy; source code, architecture, credentials, unpublished documentation (including this proposal pack); and any other information that a reasonable hospitality operator or software contractor would treat as confidential.

10.3 Exclusions

Confidential Information does not include information that the Receiving Party can show:

  • is or becomes public other than by breach of this Agreement;
  • was already in its possession without a duty of confidence;
  • is independently developed without use of the Disclosing Party’s Confidential Information; or
  • is received lawfully from a third party without a duty of confidence.

10.4 Use, care, and compelled disclosure

The Receiving Party shall (a) use Confidential Information solely for the Project; (b) disclose it only to Representatives who need it; (c) protect it with at least the same care it uses for its own similar information, and in any event no less than reasonable care; and (d) not reverse engineer software provided solely in object form except as mandatory law allows. Personal data shall be handled under the GDPR. The Operator is controller of guest data. The Contractor acts as processor only if hosting or support so requires; in that case the Parties shall sign a GDPR Article 28 data-processing agreement before any processing of live guest data.
